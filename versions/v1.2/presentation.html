06 First increment · prove the foundation

Start with a small snapshot that is useful on its own.

For one agreed customer group and selected policy categories, profile an approved sample first. Use it to shape a read-only view with source and last-updated date, while keeping unknowns visible.

What we can achieveA source-labelled prototype snapshot, a baseline of what the available data can support, and a clear decision on what to build next.
DATA FITNESS

Can we trust the records?

Check permitted access, links between customer, policyholder and covered person, and completeness of insurer, product, status, benefit and key dates.

MeasureReference-checked match accuracy; unmatched records; field completeness; freshness; duplicate and conflict rates.
CUSTOMER CLARITY

Can people understand the view?

Ask reviewers to find a policy, tell where it came from and explain what “not visible” means.

MeasureTask completion; source and status understanding; useful corrections; labels that cause confusion.
WHAT WE LEARN

What should come next?

Compare data gaps with customer and adviser feedback before expanding the scope.

LearnUsable sources; customer-declared fields; next policy category; remaining permission needs.
Set targets after profilingThis first increment can validate data fitness and comprehension. It cannot establish total cover, prove a protection gap or demonstrate sales uplift.
Presenter cue

Keep this first step narrow: an approved sample, a selected policy slice and an honest view of data confidence. Agree thresholds only after seeing the baseline.
